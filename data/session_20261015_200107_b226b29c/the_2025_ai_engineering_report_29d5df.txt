The 2025 AI Engineering Report Source: Amplify Partners, “The 2025 AI
Engineering Report” URL:
https://www.amplifypartners.com/blog-posts/the-2025-ai-engineering-report
Published: June 24, 2025 Author: Barr Yaron

Purpose Amplify Partners surveyed engineers who build AI systems. The
report discusses model usage, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG),
fine-tuning, multimodal systems, AI agents, monitoring, evaluation, and
vector databases. The findings describe the survey population and should
not be treated as universal statistics for every AI team.

Key findings - For customer-facing applications, OpenAI models account
for 3 of the 5 most popular models and 5 of the top 10. - 70% of
respondents said they use RAG in some form. - More than 50% of
respondents update their models at least monthly; 17% update weekly. -
70% update prompts at least monthly, and about 10% update prompts
daily. - 41% of respondents said they fine-tune models. Task accuracy
was the most commonly stated fine-tuning goal. Respondents mentioned
parameter-efficient techniques such as LoRA and QLoRA, as well as DPO
and reinforcement fine-tuning. - More than half of respondents use LLMs
for both internal and external use cases. Code generation and code
intelligence were prominent use cases. - 94% of LLM users use models for
at least two use cases, and 82% use them for at least three. - LangChain
was the most frequently selected framework for building generative-AI
applications, with more than twice as many respondents selecting it as
the next most popular option, LlamaIndex. - Audio had a notable
potential adoption wave: 37% of respondents who were not currently using
audio said they planned to adopt it. - 80% said LLMs were working well
at work, while fewer than 20% said the same about agents. - Among
respondents with agents in production, most reported that agents had
write access, commonly with a human in the loop. 13% said their agents
could act completely independently. - For monitoring, 60% used standard
observability, and more than 50% relied on offline evaluation. Human
review was the most popular approach for evaluating model or system
accuracy. - 65% of respondents used a dedicated vector database. - 35%
said they primarily self-hosted their vector database, while 30%
primarily used a third-party provider. - More than 40% of respondents
were not willing to pay more for inference-time compute. - Most
respondents expected open-source models to eventually converge with
closed-source models. - On average, respondents estimated that 26% of
the US Gen Z population would have an AI boyfriend or girlfriend.

Answers to evaluation questions Q: For customer-facing applications,
which company’s models dominate the top rankings? A: OpenAI. The report
says OpenAI models made up 3 of the top 5 and half of the top 10 most
popular models for customer-facing applications.

Q: What percentage of respondents are using RAG in some form? A: 70%.

Q: How often are most respondents updating their models? A: More than
50% update their models at least monthly, including 17% who update
weekly.
